# Scene Ledger Poster

Create one polished editorial poster from the supplied photograph. Treat the photograph as evidence of a real moment; the design should organize that evidence without inventing a different event.

## Visual system

- Default to a vertical 4:5 canvas unless the user requests another ratio.
- Keep one large, continuous photographic frame as the dominant element.
- Build the remaining space from a neutral paper field, a thin coordinate grid, one indexing rule, and compact metadata.
- Derive one accent color from a small but meaningful color already present in the photo.
- Use crisp rectangular crops and straight edges. Do not simulate torn paper, scrapbook tape, stickers, film borders, or distressed collage edges.
- Prefer modern grotesk typography, small caps, tabular numerals, and disciplined alignment.

## Read the source

Identify the main subject, the most important spatial relationship, the horizon or dominant axis, the native light, and one secondary detail that establishes place. Preserve these facts in the main image.

Choose a single archival idea from the visible scene, such as distance, crossing, shelter, repetition, weather, arrival, or stillness. Let that idea guide the title and metadata without fabricating a location, date, or personal story.

## Composition

1. Reserve 62–78% of the canvas for the hero photograph.
2. Place the image against a generous margin field; one side may be wider to carry indexing information.
3. Add one small detail crop only when it clarifies the scene. It must come from the supplied image.
4. Add a short title of 2–6 words. Use the user's wording when provided; otherwise derive neutral wording from visible content.
5. Add two or three factual labels, such as `SCENE`, `LIGHT`, `DIRECTION`, or `FRAME`. Never invent GPS coordinates, dates, camera settings, names, or brands.
6. Use one thin rule or coordinate marker to connect the metadata to a visible feature.

## Source protection

- Preserve recognizable people, architecture, objects, logos, and written text in the main photograph.
- Do not change identity, weather, season, location, pose, or the relationship between subjects.
- Do not add people, landmarks, products, clouds, plants, signs, or decorative objects.
- Minor exposure, white-balance, contrast, and clarity corrections are allowed when natural.
- Any secondary crop must be a truthful crop of the same source, not a regenerated substitute.

## Output check

Before returning the image, verify that the photo remains dominant, metadata is legible but subordinate, all stated facts are visible or user-provided, and the design reads as an archive page rather than an advertisement or scrapbook.

Return the finished image only unless the user asks for design notes.
